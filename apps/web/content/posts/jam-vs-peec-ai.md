**Short answer:** Both tools track how your brand shows up across several AI answer engines. Pick Peec AI if you want a focused analytics dashboard with sentiment and position metrics, multi-country tracking and a fixed monthly price per tier. Pick Jam if you want the work after tracking handled too: Jam [researches, writes and ships articles as pull requests](https://spreadjam.com/pricing) and runs email outreach from the same product. For a developer-tools startup with a docs or blog repo and a small team, that is usually the deciding difference.

## Which engines and prompts does each tool track?

Jam tracks six platforms on every plan: [ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Google AI Mode, Google AI Overviews and Microsoft Copilot](https://spreadjam.com/pricing). You do not pick a subset. The prompt count is the dial: [50 to 1,000 prompts](https://spreadjam.com/pricing), with scans weekly, every other day or daily.

Peec AI tracks more engines in total but limits how many you run at once. On [Starter, Pro and Advanced you get 3 active models](https://peec.ai/pricing), chosen from ChatGPT, AI Mode, AI Overviews, Microsoft Copilot, Gemini and Naver AI. On Peec's pricing page, Perplexity appears only in the Enterprise model list, next to API models such as Claude Sonnet 4, GPT 5 Search, Grok, Deepseek, Qwen, Mistral and Meta Spark. A separate add-on lists Perplexity among additional models, with no price shown.

This matters for developer tools. Buyers ask Perplexity and ChatGPT for tool recommendations, and a three-model cap means choosing which of those you leave unmeasured. Peec's Enterprise tier removes the cap. Jam does not list Claude among its platforms, so if Claude coverage is a hard requirement, Peec Enterprise is the option that names it.

Peec also gives you more room on geography: [1 country on Starter, 3 on Pro and Advanced, unlimited on Enterprise](https://peec.ai/pricing), with unlimited languages and competitors on every plan. Jam's pricing page does not state country or language options.

## What does each tool cost?

Peec publishes four tiers. On its pricing page, [Starter is $95/month, Pro $245/month and Advanced $495/month](https://peec.ai/pricing). Third-party roundups quote Peec in euros, for example [€89 for Starter and €199 for Pro](https://blog.timsoulo.com/14-peec-ai-alternatives-for-ai-search-visibility-tracking-2026), so the amount you see depends on currency and date.

Jam prices by prompt volume instead of fixed tiers. The slider moves through [50, 200, 400, 600, 800 and 1,000 prompts](https://spreadjam.com/pricing). At the default of 400 prompts the page shows $299/month for scans every other day across 6 platforms, with 2,000 work credits a month. Jam publishes a "Start free" option but does not state its prompt or credit limits, and it does not publish prices for the other slider positions or for Enterprise.

| Setup | Prompts | Engines | Frequency | Price |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Peec Advanced](https://peec.ai/pricing) | 350 | 3 | Daily | $495/month |
| [Jam at the default slider position](https://spreadjam.com/pricing) | 400 | 6 | Every other day | $299/month, 2,000 credits included |

Peec counts [monthly AI answers as prompts times active models times tracking frequency](https://peec.ai/pricing): 350 prompts, 3 models, 30 days gives 31,500 on Advanced. Jam at 400 prompts, 6 platforms and a scan every other day (about 15 scans in a month) works out to roughly 36,000 answers, so the volume is comparable while Jam covers every engine and Peec covers three. At the low end the comparison flips: Peec Starter at $95 for 50 prompts is the cheaper way to monitor a small question set daily.

The two models also bill differently. Peec's price buys tracking and analytics. Jam's price buys tracking plus a credit balance spent on work: [about 150 credits for a researched and written article, about 50 for a deep research report, and 10 to 40 for a chat task](https://spreadjam.com/pricing). Jam says [2,000 credits covers about 13 articles](https://spreadjam.com/pricing), and credit top-ups never expire.

## Does the tool help with content and fixes, or only tracking?

This is the largest functional gap between the two.

Peec is an analytics product. Its homepage describes [Visibility, Position and Sentiment metrics, source analysis, strategy recommendations and reporting](https://peec.ai/). Its Actions feature [clusters cited sources into owned and earned media gaps and ranks each by expected impact, with a content brief](https://www.tryprofound.com/articles/profound-vs-peec-ai). Per Profound's comparison page, [writing and publishing happen elsewhere](https://www.tryprofound.com/articles/profound-vs-peec-ai), either by hand or through an agent you connect over Peec MCP. A third-party roundup says the same: Peec has [no content creation tooling, no site audits and no traditional SEO features](https://blog.timsoulo.com/14-peec-ai-alternatives-for-ai-search-visibility-tracking-2026). Both of those pages come from sources with their own products to sell, so treat them as descriptions of scope, not a verdict on quality.

Jam closes the loop inside the product. Every plan includes [content planning, SEO data analysis, deep research reports, and articles researched, written and shipped as pull requests](https://spreadjam.com/pricing). A chat agent with 50+ tools lets you [approve drafts and review PRs from Slack and GitHub](https://spreadjam.com/). For a developer-tools team whose docs and blog live in a repository, a fix arrives as a diff you review and merge, not as a recommendation in a dashboard.

Jam also covers outbound. Its product includes [a shared inbox, lead enrichment, automatic mailbox warm-up and custom automations](https://spreadjam.com/), so visibility work and cold email run in one place. Peec does not describe an outreach product.

## Where Peec AI is stronger

- **Analytics depth.** Peec reports [Position and Sentiment](https://peec.ai/) alongside visibility. Jam's published metric is a [Visibility Score, the share of tracked prompts where you are cited](https://spreadjam.com/).
- **Reporting and data access.** Peec offers [CSV export, a Looker Studio connector and an API](https://peec.ai/), and [MCP on all plans](https://peec.ai/pricing).
- **Team size and geography.** [Unlimited users on every plan](https://peec.ai/pricing) and multi-country tracking from Pro.
- **Crawler data.** Peec's [crawlability audit covers 40+ bots, and its integrations include Vercel, Cloudflare, AWS CloudFront, Google Cloud CDN and Akamai](https://peec.ai/pricing). Jam's pricing page lists no crawler analytics.
- **Entry price.** [$95/month](https://peec.ai/pricing) for a daily 50-prompt setup.
- **Compliance.** Peec's pricing page lists [SOC 2 on all plans](https://peec.ai/pricing), while [Profound's comparison page says Peec's SOC 2 is in progress](https://www.tryprofound.com/articles/profound-vs-peec-ai). If this gates your purchase, ask Peec for its current report. Jam's pricing page states no compliance information.

## Who should pick which?

**Pick Jam if:**
- You want articles and documentation changes drafted and opened as pull requests, not only a list of gaps.
- You want every major engine, including Perplexity, tracked without choosing a subset.
- You also run cold email and want it next to your visibility data.
- Your team is small and you would rather pay for completed work through credits than hire a content owner.

**Pick Peec AI if:**
- You already have writers or an agency and need clean measurement, with sentiment, position and source reporting.
- You track several countries or need unlimited seats for a large team.
- You want the lowest-cost daily tracking for a short prompt list.
- You need Claude or other API models and can move to Enterprise.

**Use both if** you want Peec's reporting layer for stakeholders and Jam to do the content work. They do not overlap on drafting and publishing.

## FAQ

**Which tool should a developer-tools startup use in 2027?**
Choose by what happens after the report. If a human team will act on recommendations, Peec's analytics fit. If you want the fixes written and delivered to your repo, Jam does that on [every plan](https://spreadjam.com/pricing).

**Does Peec AI track Perplexity?**
On its pricing page, [Perplexity appears in the Enterprise model list](https://peec.ai/pricing). Its Starter, Pro and Advanced model list names ChatGPT, AI Mode, AI Overviews, Microsoft Copilot, Gemini and Naver AI. Jam tracks [Perplexity on every plan](https://spreadjam.com/pricing).

**Does Peec AI write content?**
No. Per [Profound's comparison](https://www.tryprofound.com/articles/profound-vs-peec-ai), Actions includes a content brief and the writing and publishing happen elsewhere.

**Is there a free option?**
Both pages show a free entry point: [Peec's "Start free trial" button](https://peec.ai/pricing) with no trial length stated, and [Jam's "Start free"](https://spreadjam.com/pricing) with no stated limits.

**How many AI answers does a plan cover?**
Peec states it: [4,500, 13,500 and 31,500 per month on Starter, Pro and Advanced](https://peec.ai/pricing). Jam does not publish an answer count, only prompts, platforms and scan frequency.

## Next steps

1. Write down the 50 to 400 buyer questions a developer would ask an AI assistant about your category.
2. Run the same list through a trial on each tool and compare which engines return your brand and which cited sources they show.
3. Count the hours your team has for turning findings into pages. If it is close to zero, price Jam's credits against the cost of a content hire.
4. Check the current numbers on the [Jam pricing page](https://spreadjam.com/pricing) and [Peec pricing page](https://peec.ai/pricing) before you buy, because both change.
